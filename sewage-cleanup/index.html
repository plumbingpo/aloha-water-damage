Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. Across Aloha, Aloha Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Clear tree roots near sewer lines, which can infiltrate and block the pipes
  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • A drain that is slowing down should be dealt with early, while it is still partly open
  • Use drain strainers in showers and sinks to catch hair and debris
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
  • Route sump pump and gutter water away from the sewer line, which can back up when storm flow overloads it

When to Call for Sewage Cleanup in Aloha

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • Strong sewage or rotten egg odors coming from drains or lower levels
  • Sewage residue visible around drain openings or on basement floors
  • Multiple drains in your home backing up at the same time
  • Toilet water with waste in it soaking into carpet or running across the floor
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain
  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.

Common Sewage Cleanup Questions

The emergency phase, meaning sewage removal plus decontamination, generally lasts 1 to 3 days. Complete restoration, including material replacement, may take 1 to 3 weeks depending on the extent of damage. Throughout, the focus is on returning your home to a safe, livable state without delay.
Keep people and pets out of the affected area. Any water sent down a drain adds to the problem, so hold off on toilets, taps and draining appliances. Turn off power and the HVAC system to the affected area only if you can do so without touching contaminated water. Skip the mop and bucket, scrub any exposed skin and wait for a trained crew.
Standard homeowner's insurance often does NOT cover sewage backup unless you have a specific sewage or water backup endorsement. The coverage usually runs only $50 to $100 per year and gives critical protection, so our strong recommendation is to add it. Check your policy or ask your agent.
Typical pricing for this work in Aloha is $2,000 to $10,000 and up, with the size of the affected area and the degree of contamination setting the number. Expect roughly $1,500 to $3,000 when the backup is minor and contained, and more than $15,000 in cases of extensive flooding that need materials replaced and rebuilding done.
Most backups start with a blockage in the home's sewer line. Typical culprits are tree roots entering the pipe, grease hardening inside it and wipes or similar items flushed down a toilet. Other possibilities are a failed old pipe and a municipal sewer overloaded by heavy rain, which sends sewage up through floor drains. A camera run through the line after cleanup identifies the actual cause.
